**Status:** the exact **spine layer**, and central to the curve work — not a
spike, and not off the W01→W04 path. It supplies the exact anchors, the proved
closure rule and the exact joins those studies draw on. It is not yet imported
by `clothoid_lab`; that import is the promotion gate, not a question about
whether the work matters.

## What is proved, not asserted

- **Closure.** With one triangle, a chain ends on an axis **iff** its total turn
  coefficient is zero (Niven: `tan` of a rational multiple of π is 0 or ±1, and
  `tan α = a/b` with `0 < a < b`). So 0° and 180° are exact; **90° is
  unreachable by accumulated turning** and needs a right-angle cusp or a global
  quarter turn.
- **Exactly closing pairs** are sparse: `T(n) = m·T(j)` gives ×2 → (3,2),(20,14);
  ×3 → (2,1),(9,5),(35,20); ×8 → (15,5),(32,11). The smooth (G2) family is
  `j(j+1) = 2n(n−1)` → n = 3, 15, 85 only.
- **Against the real engine** (`compare_bertolazzi.py`, 79-3120-3121, L=3121):
  node heading error **1e-13°**, node position error **1.0032 chords**. It is the
  Euler polygon of the clothoid with `κ₀ = α/(2L)`, `kd = α/L²` — not the
  clothoid.
- **Acceleration.** `κ = (turn/L)·α`, an exact rational multiple of α, so
  equality is decided without ever evaluating `atan`. Measured on the curl's
  entry run: the chain follows `κ = κ₀ + kd·s` to **0.8 % of peak**; the drawn
  cubics to **1.4 % (rms 0.8 %)**. It is an Euler spiral to a percent and a half.
- **The apsis** reverses curvature at its peak: signed jump 2κ, and `|κ|` never
  moves (1.00 ramp steps). That is a leaf tip, by design, and `quality.verdict`
  says so. Reported as a defect only if it exceeds the ordinary `α/L` ramp.
- **Menger curvature** (`2h/(|ab||bc|)` from three nodes) is the definition of a
  node's curvature that needs no left/right choice. A corner gets **two**
  targets, arriving and leaving. Solving the cubic's two arm lengths against
  those targets takes the worst knot error from **60–100 % to ~2 %**, and the
  corner to 0 % because each span carries its own side.
- **Fit quality.** Worst distance from the cubics to the chords: 0.029 of a
  chord, against a chain sagitta of 0.013 — the fit is at the chain's own
  resolution, i.e. ~0.09 mm on a 200 mm page.

## Decisions already taken (do not relitigate without a reason)

| decision | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| apsis | `sharp` default | the corner is the chain's own turn, to the digit; `smooth` rounds it and overshoots κ by 3× |
| arms | `arc` default, `menger` opt-in | arc gives the lower peak; menger is for when knot curvature must be right (joins) |
| Menger vs q·α | Menger = intent, q·α = ledger | Menger is geometric and side-free; q·α is exact and comparable |
| vocabulary | **"interpolated spine"**, not "Hermite" | the repo already says spine/rail in `svg.document()` |
| single-chord spans | drawn as the chord | smoothing one end of a short span overshoots κ by 2× |
| rails | miter offset, exact; taper in chords | rational normals and rational line meeting; no arc offset, no fold repair |
| volutes | arcs of shrinking chord, joined G1 | a constant-chord volute aliases at the eye |
| scale | float hybrid: exact plan, double ink | closure is a plan fact, not a coordinate fact |
| 90 degree corners | quarter-turn cusp, exact in floats too | a swap, not an angle |
| placement | rotation from the group, rational scale, integer grid | arbitrary angles are float-only |
| caps | clothoid segments quantised to the lattice | 0.44% median, 10% worst at 2-unit chords |

## Open work, in the order I would do it

1. **Exponent dial — done 2026-10-28.** `design.spiral/lobe/curl` take
   `power`; turns run `k**p` and p = 1 is the old curl bit for bit (frozen
   SHA-256 over every node plus the turn schedule, `tests/test_curve.py`).
   Every integer p still closes exactly, because the reversal cancels the same
   integers; p = 0 is a circle, p > 1 holds the turning to the last chords.
   `--power` exposes it on the CLI. **Finding:** the `chord` budget fixes the
   triangle's base angle, not the widest chord. At p = 2 the last chord turns
   ~4× the budget, so the apsis can overshoot the requested peak by up to one
   such turn (peak 120° at p = 3 lands at 194°). The next dial refinement, if
   wanted, is to solve the triangle against `(n-1)**p * alpha = chord`.
2. **`join()` / `fork()` — done 2026-10-28.** `Spiral.knots()` gives one
   `(point, exit heading)` per node, exact. `design.fork(path, node, steps,
   length, power, turn, quarter, sign)` returns the branch anchor and the
   branch itself; `turn` counts triangle angles and `quarter` right angles --
   whole numbers, with `ValueError` otherwise, never degrees. At zero it leaves
   along the shared tangent; `sign=-1` gives it the opposite curvature.
   `design.joined(a, b, node)` asserts the same rational point and the same
   exact direction (b's departure is `b.headings[0]`, not `b.heading`) and
   returns `b`. `scripts/explainer.py`'s branch figure is now drawn by these
   calls, so the figure cannot drift from the API.
3. **Float hybrid — done 2026-10-29** in `hybrid.py`, twelve tests, measured
   in `docs/EXTREMES.md`: 20,000 chords in 0.03 s, closure exact in the plan,
   ink within ~1e-13. The exact path is unchanged and still preferred for small
   chains. Remaining: a float-mode closure check that reports the ink residual
   rather than asserting, and float-native rails for production use.
4. **Approximate closure.** End within ε of horizontal instead of exactly — ten
   lines, and it unlocks a *fine* smooth apsis, which the Pell restriction
   currently forbids between n = 15 and n = 85.
5. **Placement and caps — started 2026-10-29.** `place.py` (twelve tests) and
   `docs/TRANSFER.md`. Caps transfer as measured approximations; terminals are
   blocked on a missing frame (add `anchor` and `heading` to the extractor's
   output). Next: attach a terminal once that field exists, and a cap at a
   node frame rather than at the origin.
6. **Rails and taper — done 2026-10-28** in `ribbon.py`, with ten tests; every
   study in `docs/GALLERY.md` is a filled ribbon. Still missing: a variable
   width along the spine (serration, rib), caps and round terminals, and a
   repair for a rail that folds inside a turn tighter than its half-width. The
   crossing test and the preview loop remain after that.

## Traps

- **Cost is in digits, not nodes.** Denominator is `c^T(k)`: 68 chords → 5 000
  digits → 0.07 s; 134 → 13 000 → 1.4 s; 202 → 21 000 → 9.6 s; 268 → 63 000 →
  minutes (refused by `curl`).
- **The g2 chain at n = 15 turns 20° per chord**, over the 12° smoothing
  threshold, so the default renderer draws it as a polygon. Pass
  `sharp=math.pi` or it is not a curve.
- **Never call it a clothoid.** Position error is a full chord.
- **`chord` is the base-angle budget, not the widest chord.** At `power` p the
  last chord turns `(n-1)**p` times it; check `path.chord_angle()` before
  claiming a smooth curve.
- **Cross-triangle curvature matching is impossible exactly**: `q₁α₁ = q₂α₂`
  has no rational solution in general.
- **`Spiral.cumulative()`** is the heading *of each chord*, unwrapped; the apex
  node is one past the last rising chord.
- **`Spiral.heading` is the heading *after* the last chord.** It is not the
  departure direction of a branch; that is `branch.headings[0]`. `knots()`
  exists so no caller has to remember this.

## The four questions that were still open at handover

- **Naming:** "interpolated spine" — drop "Hermite" except in one parenthesis.
- **Does it feel like an Euler?** Yes, to 1.4 % of peak on a monotone run.
- **Do floats break an exact fork?** No. Compute once, store, share.
- **Is tripling the turn the LAC?** No — geometric turns give `κ ∝ e^(λs)`,
  which is an exponential spiral. The LAC dial is the **exponent** `κ ∝ s^p`,
  with the clothoid at p = 1. Measured: all integer schedules close exactly
  under a symmetric reversal (closure is inherited); the geometric ones eat
  their turning in six chords and produce a blade, the power ones read as a
  spiral.
