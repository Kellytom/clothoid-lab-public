# pythagorean-spiral

Clothoid-shaped chains built from Pythagorean triangles, in exact rational
arithmetic. No `sin`, no `cos`, no floats in the geometry: a heading is a pair
of `Fraction`s, and every node is rational.

> **This is not a clothoid.** It is a chord polygon whose *headings* match a
> clothoid's at the nodes: ~1e-13 degrees. Everything else is different --
> curvature is a staircase rather than linear, and the position is up to a full
> chord behind the real curve. For CAD, road/rail alignment, or Class-A surface
> work, use a clothoid engine (`clothoid_lab`, pyclothoids) instead. This is a
> generator of ornamental spines, with exact rational anchors and an exact
> closure rule.

This directory is a **candidate replacement** for the curve layer, kept beside
`clothoid_lab/` so the two can be compared. Nothing here is imported by the
package yet.

**Start with the explainer:** [`docs/EXPLAINER.md`](docs/EXPLAINER.md) is the
whole story with figures, and [`docs/explainer.html`](docs/explainer.html) is
the same document with them inlined. Both are generated by
`scripts/explainer.py`, so they cannot drift.

**Then the studies:** [`docs/GALLERY.md`](docs/GALLERY.md) (HTML twin:
[gallery.html](docs/gallery.html)) puts six named objects on the layer --
catenary, drapery, Corinthian volute, violin scroll, acanthus, fleur-de-lis --
each drawn as an exact miter ribbon. Generated by `scripts/gallery.py`.

**Then the edges:** [`docs/EXTREMES.md`](docs/EXTREMES.md) (HTML twin:
[extremes.html](docs/extremes.html)) tests the hybrid at scale and a leaf with
an exact 90 degree corner. Generated by `scripts/extremes.py`.

**Then placement:** [`docs/TRANSFER.md`](docs/TRANSFER.md) (HTML twin:
[transfer.html](docs/transfer.html)) rotates inside the rational group, fits a
200 x 250 page on an integer grid, and carries the caps onto the new curve.
Generated by `scripts/transfer.py`.

## What it does

A chain is a polygon of equal chords. Chord `k` runs at heading
`alpha * T(k-1)` where `T(j) = j(j+1)/2` and `alpha` is the acute angle of a
primitive right triangle. That is the clothoid law read as a difference
equation: heading quadratic in the step index, so curvature linear in arc
length.

Three named constructions, each ending **exactly** on an axis:

| mode | turning | end | chords |
|---|---|---|---|
| `curl` | `0..n-1`, then `-(n-1)..0`, at a chosen resolution | east | 2n |
| `spiral` | `0,1,2,…` | open | n |
| `lobe` | `0..n-1`, then `-(n-1)..0` | east, sharp apsis | 2n |
| `hook --multiplier m` | `0..N`, then `-m,-2m,…` | east, sharp apsis | N+1+n_out |
| `g2` | `0..n-1`, `n-1..0`, `-1..-j` | east, **G2** apsis | 2n+j |

Exact closure is not a matter of tuning: see `docs/AUDIT.md` §3. With one
triangle the only axis landing is total turn zero, so `hook` and `g2` exist only
on the integer solutions of `T(N) = m*T(n_out)` and `j(j+1) = 2n(n-1)`.

## Making a curve rather than a polygon

Two numbers set the drawing, neither of them a hairiness knob you tune by eye:

```sh
python -m pythagorean_spiral.cli --mode curl --peak 200 --chord 6
```

`--peak 200` is the heading at the apsis, in degrees: past vertical, so the
chain rises, turns over, and comes back to an exact east exit. `--chord 6` is
the widest turn a single chord may take; that budget *is* the resolution, and
it picks the triangle (`for_angle`). 200 degrees at 6 degrees a chord gives 134
chords on 1281-820480-820481, `alpha = 0.0895°`.

The polygon is then drawn as cubics:

- spans break at a curvature reversal, so no cubic has to change its bulge
  sense inside a span;
- a node whose turn is under 12° is smoothed onto the bisector of its incoming
  and outgoing chord, so consecutive cubics meet G1 — the polygon stops
  reading as a polygon. A larger turn keeps both chord directions, so a
  deliberate corner stays a corner;
- the arms use the parabola rule, which is exact for a circular arc.

Measured on that curl: `join_kink = 0` (G1 everywhere) and the worst distance
from the cubics to the chords is `0.0129` on a figure 63 units tall — equal to
the chain's own sagitta, i.e. the fit is below the resolution of the chords it
interpolates. On a 200 mm page that is about 0.04 mm.

Exactness costs: the coordinates are integers over `c^T(steps-1)`, so a chord
budget of 6° is 13 000-digit integers and takes about a second; 4° is 21 000
digits and nine seconds. `curl` refuses budgets past ~25 000 digits rather
than hang, and 6° is already at the floor of what the page can show.

## What is exact, and what is not

Exact: the triangle rotations, the node coordinates, the turn bookkeeping, the
closure conditions, the final heading (it is literally east, not 0.000°).

Not exact: the curve. The chain is the **Euler polygon** of the clothoid with
`kappa0 = alpha/(2L)`, `kd = alpha/L²`. Measured against Bertolazzi on the
79-3120-3121 chain: node heading error `1e-13` degrees, node position error up
to `1.0032` chords. So the headings are the clothoid's, and the vertices are a
polygon that tracks it.

The chain stops resolving a curve when `alpha*k` approaches a radian, around
`k = 1/alpha = 39` steps for the thin triangle. Past that the chords jump the
local radius and the picture is aliasing, not a spiral. The CLI prints the
widest chord angle and warns past ~52°:

| construction | widest chord angle | reads as a curve |
|---|---|---|
| `lobe --steps 7` | 8.7° | yes |
| `lobe --steps 15` | 20.3° | yes |
| `hook --multiplier 3 --n-out 5` | 21.8° | yes |
| `hook --multiplier 8` | 58.0° | no — the fast exit aliases |
| `spiral --steps 32` | 45.0° | marginal, two laps |
