W01–W02 — the piece model and terminals

One drawing, several traces, one mirror — and a vocabulary for how each ribbon ends.

What you are looking at

  • ink/half — one clothoid chain, drawn as the centre line of a ribbon.
  • ink/half rail ±3 — the two edges, each offset 3 mm from the centre line; together they make a 6 mm ribbon.
  • ink/half mirrored — the same three paths reflected across x = 0. The mirror is one setting on the piece, not a second drawing.
  • guide/band — a straight guide, explicitly not mirrored, so it does not draw twice.

What this proves

The engine still draws one chain at a time. A Piece holds named traces, each with its own Design, optional ribbon width and mirror rule. render_piece() builds every trace, mirrors the groups that ask for it, and writes one SVG with one page, one frame and one y-flip. The path ids are the trace names, so a later grammar can address a band or a petal directly.

The terminal strip is the W02 vocabulary, eight millimetres wide, the same on both ends: butt stops the rails where they are; point brings them to a sharp tip; arc is a shallow five-millimetre dome; round is a semicircle of half the width; taper converges over four half-widths; bulb and cone are the two width laws recovered from the ornaments stage-two page, w = ½(t(3−3t+t²)+12t²(1−t)³) and w = ½(1−(1−t)³). Both meet the full stroke at the join with zero slope and zero curvature. Each end is set on the trace and reported per end with its kind and reach, and a terminal refuses a ribbon that folds.
